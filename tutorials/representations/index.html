Representations¶

Irrep[g, w] builds the irreducible representation of g with highest weight w (Dynkin labels). From it you read off the dimension, the weight system, the Casimir eigenvalue, and the explicit Chevalley generator matrices — for every classical type, including the orthogonal/symplectic spinors that do not live in tensor powers of the defining representation. Mirrors demos/02-representations.wls.

Needs["ClassicalLieAlgebra`"];

One irrep, end to end: the su(3) adjoint¶

The su(3) adjoint is the highest weight {1, 1} — the “octet” of the eightfold way:

ir = Irrep[SU[3], {1, 1}];
HighestWeight[ir]            (* {1, 1} *)
RepresentationDimension[ir]  (* 8, from the Weyl dimension formula *)
CasimirEigenvalue[ir]        (* 6 *)

WeightSystem gives the weights (as Dynkin labels) with their multiplicities. The zero weight is doubly degenerate — that is the two-dimensional center of the octet:

WeightSystem[ir]

<|{1, 1} -> 1, {-1, 2} -> 1, {2, -1} -> 1, {0, 0} -> 2,
  {-2, 1} -> 1, {1, -2} -> 1, {-1, -1} -> 1|>

The multiplicities sum to the dimension, \(1+1+1+2+1+1+1 = 8\).

RepresentationMatrices gives the Chevalley generators as explicit matrices in the irrep, in an orthonormal basis where \(E_i=F_i^\dagger\) and each \(H_i\) is diagonal:

m = RepresentationMatrices[ir];
Keys[m]                       (* {"Cartan", "Raising", "Lowering"} *)
Length /@ Values[m]           (* {2, 2, 2}: one H, E, F per simple root *)
Dimensions[m["Cartan"][[1]]]  (* {8, 8} *)
Diagonal[m["Cartan"][[1]]]    (* {1, -1, 2, 0, 0, -2, 1, -1} *)
m["Raising"][[1]] == ConjugateTranspose[m["Lowering"][[1]]]  (* True *)

Reaching the spinors¶

The so(5) spinor is 4-dimensional and does not appear in any tensor power of the 5-dimensional vector representation. The abstract highest-weight (Shapovalov) construction reaches it anyway:

sp5 = Irrep[SO[5], {0, 1}];
RepresentationDimension[sp5]                 (* 4 *)
Dimensions[RepresentationMatrices[sp5]["Cartan"][[1]]]  (* {4, 4} *)

The built matrices really satisfy the algebra. Defining chevalleyHolds[g, w] to check the relations \([E_i,F_j]=\delta_{ij}H_i\) and \([H_i,E_j]=A_{ji}E_j\) numerically:

chevalleyHolds[SU[3], {1, 1}]    (* True: the octet *)
chevalleyHolds[SO[5], {0, 1}]    (* True: the so(5) spinor *)
chevalleyHolds[SO[7], {0, 0, 1}] (* True: the so(7) 8-dim spinor *)

(The full chevalleyHolds definition is in the demo script and the physics-applications tutorial.)

Warning

Large irreps are expensive RepresentationMatrices builds exact matrices via the highest-weight / Shapovalov construction; the dimension — and the cost — grows quickly with the Dynkin labels and the rank. Build small irreps, or wrap larger ones in TimeConstrained. RepresentationDimension, CasimirEigenvalue, and WeightSystem are cheap by comparison (no matrices are constructed).
